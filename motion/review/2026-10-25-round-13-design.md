# Round 13 design: the binned Firms view, the comparison axis, the result style

The author's requests of 25 October 2026, after part D: (1) the Firms view's top level as a mosaic of binned tiles that refine with the zoom and hand over to the dots, insightful and fitting the atlas's design language, the Cells and Firms views made to match where they can; (2) the comparison beat's y-axis trimmed in both directions, the two panels kept on one scale; (3) a go-to colour and format for the video's prominently displayed empirical results, a gold in a subtle, not harshly bordered box. This note records the design before the build; the README's round-13 section records what was built and measured.

## 1. The binned Firms view

**What exists.** Below the dots zoom (k < 20 px per km) the Firms view draws the year's registered addresses as an additive dot field (round 11): 1 px marks per address, 2 by 2 and 3 by 3 blocks for 10 and 100 firms, accumulated with a log tone curve, each coloured by the layer's class at the address, under the listed marks and above the glow and the region tint. The Cells view draws shipped aggregates on a ladder of grids: 100 km cells, 25 km cells (every year), the national 5 km grid (2021 only), the 1 km city windows (22 cities, 2019 to 2024) and the 250 m Moscow window (2021), with 12 percent gaps and hand-over crossfades by zoom.

**The change, as built on 25 October.** Below the dots zoom the Firms view bins the year's addresses on the Cells view's grids and draws the bins as gapped squares, one colour per square from the layer's statistic over the addresses inside it: 25 km squares at the country (the national 25 km grid), 1 km squares from k 1.5 (the city windows' grid, here everywhere), the dots from k 20 as today. The 5 km level of the first draft was dropped: the Cells view shows 25 km cells until its 1 km windows arrive at k 1.5 to 2.5, so a 5 km level in between would have broken the match between the views. The hand-overs are crossfades over k 1.5 to 2.5 (as the Cells view's windows arrive) and over k 20 to 28 (the field canvas's opacity rising as the squares fade). The grids share the km plane's origin, so a flip between the views at any zoom shows the same squares; where the Cells view has no grid (1 km outside the 22 windows), the Firms view's squares stand alone. The 250 m level is not offered: the dots from k 20 are what the author singled out at the city zoom.

**The statistic per square, by layer** (the same breaks and colours as the Cells view's layer, from the address records: n firms, p coordinate pairs, a with a statement, f filers with a class, rv the log code of the summed revenue, the eight (division, share) pairs of the address's revenue mix).
- Share: a over n, the six-class cyan ramp with the year breaks at 25 km (fifty firms for a colour, the below tone otherwise); at 1 km the window rule, five classes over squares with 50 or more firms.
- Density: n, the eight count classes in the density colour at the count alphas, the video's look.
- Industry: the class with the largest summed revenue over the square's addresses (each address's revenue, 10^((rv − 1)/100), split by its mix shares), in the lit nine; the hover computes the leading division itself from the addresses inside the square. No revenue, the below tone.
- Revenue change (fin): the filer-weighted median of the addresses' classes, in the amber and cyan pair; no filers, the zero slate. An approximation of the Cells view's cohort median, named in the legend.
- Change, cohort and lists: no address-level analogue; the squares take the count classes in the density colour and the listed marks carry the layer.

**The population, stated.** The address file holds firms on no list with house- or street-level geocodes; the Cells view counts every located eligible firm, listed firms and city-level geocodes included. The legend of every binned level says "Firms at registered addresses, YEAR (N km squares)" with "firms on no list" in the colour line and ends "Each square bins the firms on no list at the addresses inside it. Zoom in past 20 px per km for the dots."

**Hover and panel.** At the binned levels the tile reports the square under the cursor (its firms on no list at their coordinate pairs, with a statement and the share; the leading division and its share; the filers' median class), the panel the same rows with a Square row, the mix box the square's divisions by revenue share ("1 km square at the cursor"); the listed marks still win the hover and keep the square's line. At the dots the address hover returns unchanged. The firms-in-view footer count is unchanged.

**Cost, measured at 1,600 px on WebGL.** One binning pass per year and level (92,883 1 km and 6,912 25 km squares in 2024, both summing to the year's 2,884,476 firms on no list), cached with the year's addresses. Squares of 6 px and more, and the 25 km level, are filled per frame inside the view (the bins sorted by column, the x-band cut by a binary search, one path per class); smaller squares are rendered once into a bitmap of the view with a margin of its own size and drawn through the nine-argument drawImage, so a frame costs the view's area. Frame cost: 4.8 ms mean at the country (10.6 max), 7.5 in the crossfade at k 2.2, 4.7 at k 3, 1.8 at the Moscow preset, 2.4 at k 24, 2.2 at Central Moscow. The additive field is no longer drawn below k 20; `fieldRegime()` reports the level.

**Audit.** The field states expect the level (25 at the country, 1 in the band and at the city preset, the dots' over field from Central Moscow, the crossfade at k 24); `mosaic` states per renderer assert the level, the mode, the alphas, the field's opacity, the sums, the tile's and the legend's words and the cost at five zooms; the round-10 contrast check is carried to the squares; the mix box's address state moved to the dots zoom and a square state sits at Moscow. Frames: Russia 2024 on share, density and industry; k 3; the Moscow preset in both views; k 12 and k 24; the same at 400 px.

**Open to the author.** Whether the dots should wait for k 48 with a 250 m level between (the Central Moscow preset would then show squares on screens under 960 px tall).

Decided on 25 October 2026 (evening): the dots stay at k 20 as built and no 250 m level is added. One consequence for phones, fixed the same evening: a 400 px map's fine rung lands at k 18.3, under the hand-over, so the hand-over starts at the fine rung divided by 1.4 where that is lower than 20, keeping the band's ratio; every map 768 px and wider keeps 20 to 28.

## 2. The comparison beat's y-axis

Today both panels share a fixed log domain of 10 to 500 with ticks at 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400, while the data span 25.4 (the lower quartile of the firms listed in 2022, in 2014) to 223.4 (the upper quartile of the firms on no list, in 2014) and the two firms 62 to 151. The domain becomes data-driven and still paired: the lower bound the largest of 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40 and 50 at or below 0.9 times the smallest value drawn in either panel (a quartile or a firm's index), the upper bound the smallest of 150, 200, 250, 300, 400 and 500 at or above 1.1 times the largest; the ticks the members of 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400 inside it, 100 the reference line as before. With the shipped data this is 20 to 250 with ticks at 25, 50, 100 and 200: the lines gain about 55 percent of the panel's height. Both cuts re-render; the text audit's label positions move with the axis.

## 3. The result style in the video

**The colour (the author's decision of 25 October).** No new gold: the result colour is the amber the video already has for its anchor firm, `--anchor-a` #F2B134, kept as it is. In a frame that also shows the diverging pair's amber (the change map's declines, the Moscow section's revenue classes) the result takes the ink #ECEEF1 with the circle marker instead, as Part B's item 2 proposes for Aeroflot's line there, so that one amber never means two things in one frame. The validator's figures for the anchor amber against the pair's amber #FFB000 and the listed red are recorded with the token in the README.

**The box.** The amber at 10 percent over the stage as the fill, a 1 px border in the amber at 30 percent, squared, 24 px of padding, no shadow; the figure the box delivers in the full amber (the ink with the circle marker where the rule above applies), the label and the interval in the ink and the muted tone as now. A result box is never drawn over the map's data and never on the atlas (the atlas keeps its two accents).

**Where.** One box per scene that delivers a figure: the archive's share of records with a statement (the 72 px figure), the change map's two shares and their difference (the ink with the marker, since the declines are amber there), the size scene's ratio of odds ratios with its interval, the lists scene's two odds ratios, the comparison beat's gap facts (one box per panel, the ink with the marker in the panel beside Aeroflot's amber line), the Moscow close's count line. The titles, the paragraphs and the charts keep their colours; a frame per box goes to the author before the renders.

**Audit.** The text audit's `panel-clip` rule covers the boxes (`Motion.panel` records them); the contrast of the ink and the amber on the box's fill is checked with the validator's `contrast`.

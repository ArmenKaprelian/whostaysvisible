4 · Condensing CORRECTIONS

Your rule allows one entry per correction and 8 in all, never dropping one. About 28 of round 17’s proposed fixes qualify, so the rule as it stands would stop the audit at the ninth entry the first time they are published (29 entries: 29 entries, more than CORRECTIONS_MAX (8): every correction stays, so the author decides how to condense). The drafts below are dated 30 October 2026 for the example; their counts are the audit’s, final counts come with the fixes. Both pass the audit’s own check: one entry per publish, 2 entries: pass; one per kind, 7 entries: pass.

One entry per publish keeps every correction on the page inside its publish’s entry, by kind and count, and the round’s README record, now public, holds the detail; eight entries then cover eight correcting publishes. One per kind is more specific but uses six of the eight places at once, so the next correcting publish would need condensing again. Applying either changes the rule’s wording in the README’s “A correction” and in the principles; the check and its limits stay, and a test that no two entries share a date would fit the first.

Recommendation

Medium
Likely regret
Medium: a reader sees kinds and counts, not each correction, unless they follow the record.

## Item 1 in detail

- **What the lists look like on the ground.** Of 3,550 sanctions-list firms in the 2021 annual eligible population, 3,466 are located; of 982 government strategic list firms, 953; 507 located firms are on both lists. The sanctions list occupies 341 of the 3,006 populated 50 km cells, the government strategic list 184. One Moscow cell holds 1,145 sanctions-list firms (33% of the located members) and the top two cells (Moscow and St Petersburg, 516) hold 48%; 154 cells hold one firm and 232 hold three or fewer. The government strategic list repeats the pattern at a third of the scale: 199 and 110 in the two largest cells, 78 single-firm cells. This concentration is the reason the list comparisons have thin cluster support (review Point 20, 06 line 11), and it is what the overlay shows immediately.
- **Marks.** Circle area is proportional to the count, radius 0.42 pt times the square root of the count with a floor of 0.95 pt, so cells with one to five firms share the smallest mark (the note says so). The reference circles in the legend row are 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 firms. Discs have a 0.5 pt white border and rings a white halo so they separate from the navy points. Marks are page-sized symbols, not ground areas: the same circle is 0.37 in across on the national panel and in the insets.
- **A1 against A2.** A1 (one panel, two marks) puts both lists on one map, which is what the review asked for, and leaves half the page free; its weakness is that below about ten firms per cell a ring and a disc look alike, and in Moscow the rings sit inside the discs. A2 (two panels, one list each) removes that ambiguity and uses the production geometry unchanged, so it is the easiest for Codex to re-render; it costs the whole page. B (both lists over the current available and absent panels) answers four questions on one page, repeats the overlay twice and keeps the absent panel that, as the review notes, adds little beyond the available one.
- **Navy against phthalo green under the brick marks (the author's request).** The green renders (`a1-one-panel-green`, `a2-two-panels-green`) are complete and pass every check. The brick marks separate less from the dark green than from navy because both colours are dark and the hue contrast is smaller, and the figure loses the navy it shares with Figure 2.8 on the same frame. The palette rule (`LaTeX/AGENTS.md` line 78) pairs navy and phthalo green to distinguish two series or cohorts; here the population is one series and the lists are an overlay in the established overlay colour, so navy is the rule-conforming choice. Green would be worth considering only if the author wants Figure 2.7's population to contrast with Figure 2.8's cohort cells rather than match them.
- **Insets.** The window counts are all located firms in the 90 km windows (Moscow 765,238, St Petersburg 262,680, the sums of the production's available and absent counts). Marks in the insets are the same 50 km cell circles clipped to the window; they locate the cell, not the firm.
- **Geometry and text.** The national panel keeps the production projection, extents and inset windows; the fidelity render `s0-current` reproduces the manuscript's national panel pixel for pixel at 200 dpi with identical text (the only difference is the embedded font, Liberation Sans in the manuscript copy against Nimbus Sans in the lab). Every candidate drops the "01" prefix and draws all text at 10.5 pt or larger (titles 11.5 pt bold); the current figure draws its notes at 8.3 pt and its inset labels at 8.1 pt. The one-panel pages are 6.5 x 4.6 in, the two-panel pages 6.5 x 7 in with the national band reduced from 2.52 to 2.45 in (A2), 2.35 in (B) or 2.25 in (composite) to make room for the legend rows and four or five note lines at the floor size. Lines that the PDF no longer carries (overlap hides multiplicity, insets repeat firms, registered addresses are not operating locations) are in each candidate's `-notes.tex` snippet for the LaTeX figure note.

## Item 2 in detail

- **Construction.** From the saved annual 100 km cells of the pass-2 reconciliation (`fig27.cells-years`), 2024 availability share minus 2021 share per cell, in percentage points, on the 658 cells with at least 50 located eligible firms in both years; 9 cells supported only in 2021 and 12 only in 2024 are gray with the rest. Located shares: 64.0% in 2021 and 66.5% in 2024 nationally, 64.0% and 66.6% within the supported cells (63.99 and 66.58), so the national change is +2.5 points and the map shows its geography. The cell changes run from −47 to +20 points, with the extremes in small cells.
- **Classes.** Six symmetric classes at ±2 and ±5 points, 15 km gaps between the 100 km tiles (the author's Figure 2.8 decision), unsupported cells in the production gray. Supported cells per class: under −5, 73; −5 to −2, 82; −2 to 0, 109; 0 to +2, 125; +2 to +5, 145; over +5, 124. Three round breaks keep the legend readable; an eight-class version with ±10 would add 17 and 23 cells at the ends and two more swatches. Zero is a class boundary, so no cell is coloured as unchanged and the two lightest classes carry the sign.
- **Colours.** The navy side is the Figure 2.8 study's classed navy with a lighter first step (`#A9C4DE #6E9AC2 #2F6395`); the brick side is the same three lightness and chroma values with the hue of the warm brick, built with the study's `fig28_match_hue()`. The dataviz validator passes each side as an ordinal ramp; the all-pairs check over the six classes is recorded in `data/fig27/fig27-change-palette.provenance.json` and on the gallery page. In grayscale the two sides merge at equal lightness, which is the usual limit of a diverging map; the sign is carried by hue.
- **The note.** The PDF carries the sentence that the change compares the 2021 and 2024 annual populations at registered addresses, not firms, and the `-notes.tex` snippet repeats it with the class definition.
- **Reading.** Most dark navy cells (gains over 5 points) sit in central European Russia and the Urals; the dark brick cells (losses over 5 points) cluster in the south-west and along the southern border of Siberia, with mixed cells in the Far East. The author should read the pattern on the map before any sentence is written about it; the lab has computed no regional summary.

## Item 3 in detail

- **Two branches, never one funnel.** Both diagrams start from the annual eligible population and split: the left branch pairs the firms registered at the year-end with the following year (transition population, resolved states, survivor population, financial-retention samples), the right branch takes the 2021 records through the cohort entry requirements to the four fixed 2021 cohorts and their annual dispositions. Transition counts are firm-year pairs; cohort counts are firms. Navy marks the Chapter 2 branch and phthalo green the Chapter 3 branch, the palette rule's pair for two adjacent series.
- **Counts.** Every count printed in either diagram occurs verbatim in the pinned manuscript files (`02-data-and-samples.tex`, `05-results.tex`, `07-financial-sample-implications.tex`, `b-chapter2-evidence.tex`); the 44 checks in `data/flow/flow-nodes.checks.csv` test each string and six arithmetic identities (the prior-state rows of Table B.2 sum to their denominators, the survivor population is the available plus absent survivors, the removals sum to 1,702,040, the survivor model's retained sample is the population less 79,146, and the identity screen and statement requirement leave 2,129,174). ## Item 3, second round: the flowchart (`flow2-tikz`, `flow2-r`)

- **What changed.** Every box now carries only a registry name, one or two count lines and, in gray with a marker, the model estimated on that population (eligible annual model, registered annual model, survivor model, size models and the six financial specifications, Chapter 3 regressions). The definitions left the figure; they belong to the registry's glossary table, which is the unified standard the author wants reinforced, so the figure and the table use one vocabulary. Each bar block has one table of names and counts under it, so no count depends on reading a width. - **What the bars show.** Prior observers are almost all available again (9,323,396 of 10,484,716), prior absentees mostly absent again (2,949,830 of 4,814,211) or removed (1,401,172); the reporting-scope changes and unresolved cases are too few to draw and appear only in the table. The exact exclusion counts come from Appendix Figure B.2 (`stage3-b-cohort-accounting-2.pdf`), which the lab pinned and reads with pdftotext in the checks; the prose rounds two of them ("about 46,500", "about 370,000").

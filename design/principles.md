# Armen G. Kaprelian: design preferences and principles

Written in the dataviz lab on 25 October 2026 from the records of rounds 1 to 11 of the motion study, and brought up to round 16 on 27 October 2026: the plan file (`~/.claude/plans/can-you-within-your-elegant-lantern.md`), the round sections of `motion/README.md`, the decision log of the lab `README.md`, the memory, and the author's corrections at each checkpoint. Every principle below is paired with the decision that produced it and, where one exists, the option rejected and why. Dates are 2026. "The author" is Armen G. Kaprelian; the lab is Claude's.

## Summary (one page)

The work is a dark, flat, monospace system in which colour means one thing at a time and nothing on screen is decoration.

- **Identity.** One mark (four corner brackets and a dot), one typeface (IBM Plex Mono at 400, 500 and 700), capitals for names and interface labels with 8 percent tracking, the striped display style on large type only. Symbols are never striped, except the REFERENCES icon (round 16). Two exceptions to the one typeface: the data wire's track in Tomorrow (round 15) and DejaVu Sans Mono for the Armenian letters of the SUPERUSER note (round 16). (Round 10, 25 October.)
- **Colour.** A near-black navy stage with the land as a lit shape on it; two accents with fixed roles, fuchsia where a control acts on the map and neon green where a panel only reads, both at full strength at rest and brighter only while something moves or has focus; one exception, the ice blue of the GO TO card, because travel neither acts on the data nor reads it; the listed red reserved for firms on the five lists and the one red control that belongs to them; a single-hue cyan ramp for ordered quantities; one amber and cyan pair for signed change; nine lit industry hues validated pairwise; the accents never on data, ramps or the red; on the light theme a near-white sea with Russia a pale cool shape, and a thin ink edge where a fill is faint against the land. (Rounds 9, 10 and 15.)
- **Motion.** Motion reveals data or moves the camera, nothing else. Camera moves are pure functions of time and zoom toward their subject. The wire's phosphor glow is the one glow (the author's exception, round 15), shared since round 16 by the REFERENCES icon; the pulse has none since round 15. The REFERENCES panel opens and closes in under 0.2 s at the author's request. Under reduced motion everything jumps, holds or crossfades, and no loop runs. The wire scrolls on through context changes and never stops or restarts for a hover (round 16); only a year change rebuilds it, under a crossfade. City names lie on the ground and grow with the land (round 16). (Rounds 1, 6, 8, 9, 11, 16.)
- **Text.** Three tones: ink #ECEEF1, muted #B4C0D0, dim #7D889C. Descriptive wording, the chapter's own words, no em-dashes, no semicolons, every rate with its denominator; units keep their case inside tracked capitals. The muted tone was lifted one step on 25 October so that 12 px provenance reads on the panel. No "listed" on screen for list membership; methods in standard econometric terms, by the book. (Rounds 3, 9, 10, 15.)
- **Layout.** Borders by role, squared corners everywhere but the round listed-state glyphs, a fine grain on every chrome surface and never on the map, one collapse convention (a square toggle, remembered per viewer), every panel's title row on a bar with its i and its toggle, the header as three groups that never touch, the phone as a scrolling page with stacked cards. (Rounds 6, 10, 11.)
- **Data honesty.** Every figure on screen comes from a checked file and can be re-derived; the wording is descriptive, never causal; identity is never carried by colour alone; the audit at five widths on both renderers (one run of `audit-five.sh`) must report zero findings before anything is published, and the author's word precedes every publish. (Every round.)
- **Rejected.** A left-edge bar on buttons; muted accents at rest; dragging windows; the Syne title face; a third accent; white body text; a news feed in the wire; the pulse's glow and pale core, and its 44 to 64 px ring (round 22); the cursor crosshair (round 22); a lighter dark land (round 22, the red under 3:1); "firms on no exceptions list"; rings on every faint dot.

## 1. Identity

**The mark.** Four L-shaped corner brackets of arm 6 and stroke 2 in a 24-unit square with a dot of radius 2.5 at the centre, drawn in the current colour (the author's item 14b, 25 October). Where it appears, and nowhere else: before the explainer's page title, in the video credits before the name line, as the focus corners on every button and toggle of both pages, as the minimap's Russia frame, as the wire's separator (14 px with 2 px strokes since round 11), as the year slider's thumb (20 px, 2.5 px strokes, a 4 px dot, in the neon green), at 32 px in the header lock-up beside the name (round 11, item 8), and with the initials inside its square at 4 percent under every data layer of the map and the video stage (item 17). Never on the map at a data location, where it would read as a marker. The atlas title lost its mark on 25 October (round 11) when the lock-up took it.

**One typeface.** IBM Plex Mono at 400 for prose, 500 for labels and figures, 700 for headings, years and counters; tabular figures throughout; installed locally from the pinned files for the headless renders and served to the published pages from Google Fonts with the local family first in the stack (round 10, T1). Decision: the author asked for one typeface everywhere. Rejected: the Syne display face for titles, offered in the plan as an experiment with a title-face flag; dropped by the author on 25 October ("one typeface, no flag, no pin, no install"). Rejected earlier: Nimbus Sans with Arimo as the web fallback (rounds 1 to 9), replaced because a monospace sets every figure tabular by construction and the labels read as one system. The one exception (round 15, the author's final choice of 26 October 2026): the data wire's track in Tomorrow at 13.5 px, 500 for the tracked capitals and 400 for firm names and cat facts, with horizontal scanlines and the phosphor glow; pinned and installed like Plex and served from Google Fonts; the lead slot, the separators and everything else stay in Plex Mono. The second exception (round 16): Plex Mono has no Armenian letters, so the REFERENCES panel's SUPERUSER note sets them in DejaVu Sans Mono, which the published page ships in `fonts/` with its licence.

**The capitals rule.** Proper names first (the page title, the scene titles, the hook, the credits title, the place names on the map and in the location, region and strip lines), then every interface label (buttons, the year label and value, the legend title and rows, the panel row labels, the tips, the mix title, the minimap labels), in Plex Mono 500 with 8 percent tracking (round 10, T4, widened by the author on 25 October). Mixed case stays only for running prose, firm names, legal-form tags and unit symbols: a unit keeps its case inside a tracked label through a span, since "M" or "KM" read as other symbols. The DOM chapter buttons of the explainer are navigation, not titles, and keep mixed case. Rejected: synthesised small capitals (Plex Mono has no small-cap glyphs and synthesised ones break the monospace rhythm), so "small capitals" is set as tracked capitals at the small size.

**The striped display type and its exclusions.** Large type is cut by horizontal hairline gaps: eight bars per capital height on canvas (1 px gaps on the atlas, 2 px in the video), a 3 px pitch on the page (a fractional pitch blurs under the mask's antialiasing, a recorded deviation). Where: VISIBILITY ATLAS and the explainer's title line, the scene titles and the hook, the large year in the video's location panel; the legend's ramp bars are the one striped non-text element (round 10, S18). Excluded, by the author's correction of 25 October: the mark, the listed-state glyphs, the pulse button's ring and dot, the region glyphs, the minimap's dots and frame and any future symbol are drawn whole; the year value is set at 16 px, under the display floor, unstriped. The rule: stripes belong to display type and to buttons' text at display size, never to a symbol. The one exception is the author's request of 26 October 2026 (General 7, round 16): the REFERENCES panel's icon, the green i meshed with a wire globe, carries the wire's scanlines and glow, off on the light theme and under the contrast settings.

## 2. Colour

**The ground.** The stage is a near-black navy, #03060E (OKLab L 0.122; the author's pick 2C of 26 October 2026, #060B18 at L 0.152 from round 9 before; near black the land's contrast barely moves, 1.36:1 on the old stage and 1.45:1 even on black, so the pick was a look on the author's screens), and the land a lit shape on it, #172A45 (L 0.284); the panels sit 0.026 above the stage in lightness, the grid and borders rescaled by the same rule, the graticule a whiter 1 px line #2E333D that competes with neither the outline nor the data (round 9, the author's decision after the first palette frames). Rejected: the charcoal ground of rounds 3 to 8 (#121418 and #22262D), chosen then from four Central Moscow renders over a navy field, a grey or cream ground and phthalo green; and the round-2 navy (#0B1A2E and #1E2E42), rejected in round 9 because the listed red fell to 2.9:1 on that land, under the 3:1 floor the quiet national marks rely on. The light theme keeps its own tokens and is never the default: the atlas opens dark on every device whatever the browser's scheme, the button still switches (round 10, C6); the explainer has one dark theme because its rasters and renders are made for the dark stage. The light theme's own ground (round 15, the author's requests and revisions of 26 and 27 October 2026): the sea the near white #FBFBF9 and Russia a pale cool shape #D7DCE0 (OKLab L 0.892), on the map and in the minimap, the page around the map taking the sea as on the dark theme; panels #F7F8FA with role tints (rose where you act, green where you read; GO TO takes the sea so its minimap is the map in small); bands #E2EEFE; the ghost tone #C3C8CC and the grey of cells with too few firms #B9BEBB, both moved with the land to keep their steps; the share ramp's palest step lifted to #E0EFFC so it stays visible on the land. Where a fill is faint against the land, a hairline in the ink at 60 percent (3.4:1) gives it an edge: round every cell under 3:1, inside every dot of 2 px radius or more under 1.5:1; the colours stay as they are and the dark theme has none.

**Two accents and their roles.** Fuchsia (`--cursor` #FF45C8, live #FF8FDF) for everything that acts on the map or follows the cursor: the tile's border, the hover ring cased in the stage colour, the focus corners, the LAYER card and its buttons, the zoom box, the theme button. Neon green (`--context` #4DFF7F, live #9AFFB8) for everything that reads: the wire and its glyph, the tile footer's figures, the year value and the slider's mark, the Under the cursor and chart cards, the legend, the note, the mix box, the scale bar, the card and overlay titles, the METHOD button (the only green button). Both validated for AA on the stage, the land and the panel and for colour-vision distance from the listed red and from each other (fuchsia 22 normal, 25 protan, 21 deutan from the red; green 49, 48, 28; 51, 41, 24 between them). The rule, in the author's words: fuchsia you act on, green you read; two accents only. Rejected: muted resting variants (`--cursor-muted`, `--context-muted`), the lab's first proposal in round 9, corrected the same day: "neon means neon", the full colours rest and the live variants show only while something moves or has focus. Rejected: a third accent (the GO TO card first took the ink as a neutral, then a blue, never a third accent role).

**The ice exception for travel.** The GO TO card, its buttons and the minimap take a pale blue, #9CC8FF for the dots and strokes and the card's border, #CFE6FF for the leaders, with the pressed city in a solid fill and stage-coloured text (round 10, N21 and the minimap correction of 25 October). The reason, recorded with the exception: travel neither acts on the data nor reads it, so it gets the page's neutral; the legend and every other panel keep their role colours. The first neutral was the ink white; the author moved it to an ice blue on the white side (#CFE6FF), validated against the cyan ramp's pale end, the Manufacturing blue and both accents (no pale blue-white clears 15 from the ramp's pale end, so the author's value stood and the pair is recorded), then colder (#9CC8FF, 15.5 from the pale end). The selected preset shows in the full neon green until the camera leaves it; hover lights the route to the live green with the name kept in the ink; keyboard focus keeps the fuchsia frame. Round 14 (25 October 2026) extends the exception twice: the cursor tile's text takes the same ice (the author's request: "the same ice-blue as the border of the GO TO menu"), while the tile's footer keeps the reading green and the listed count its red; and the GO TO card's search by name and its link to the view are travel controls in the card's ice. Round 15: the minimap's four corner marks take the neon green at every view (the author's general point 4: an accent may depart from a functional definition for the look); the tile's text has its own token, `--tile-ink` #CFE6FF (6D), apart from the card's #9CC8FF; the GO TO card's i is green like every i.

**The listed red.** #E31A1C for firms on the five lists and nothing else: solid with a statement, hollow without one that year, a thin rim before the listing year (round 7), quieter with the zoom (rounds 8 and 11: alpha 0.60 and a 0.85 px floor at the country, the halo gone there) and the bloom at the listing year. Round 14 (B″, the author's verdict that the marks were "a bit overemphasized" at the country and the city, "though I don't want them to disappear from view"): in the atlas the resting alpha 0.48 and the floor 0.70 px at the country, and a casing in the stage colour in place of the pale halo, rising from k 20 to 60; the video keeps round 11's marks. Rejected: drawing the marks as pixel squares like the field (variant C), which made them louder. The one red figure in text is the Cells tile's listed count, a bold 19 px number after the legend's dot (4.0:1 on the dark tile, large text). The one red control is the pulse button, "because it belongs to the red marks" (25 October); every other control takes a role colour. The red is never a class colour and the chrome never uses it. Rejected: a dark purple for "statement absent" marks of firms on no list (round 3, change 1): the state is still computed but not drawn.

**The cyan ramp.** Six steps at even OKLCH lightness 0.556 to 0.940 with the hue sliding from 195 to 172 degrees (#1C8383 to #C7F8E7), validated as an ordinal ramp on the land and the stage (round 10, C5). The accent is step 4, the accent-strong step 6, so every chart reads the same family; the light theme keeps the manuscript's navy ramp. Rejected: YlGnBu (rounds 2 to 9), a multi-hue ramp that failed the one-hue check by design; the author asked for a single hue on the dark theme.

The author's orange was approximated and the amber kept exact; the outer ends differ in lightness by 0.06 because the amber half is held down by the red, stated in the record. Rejected: PRGn purple and green (rounds 2 to 9), replaced so that the signed layers share one pair with the ramp's own family.

**The lit industry nine.** The author's nine hues moved inside their semantic windows by the validator's coordinate search so that every pair among the nine, the neutral and the red is at least 13.9 normal and 8.5 colour-vision apart (Agriculture #E8B533, Manufacturing #2882F9, Construction #ED7D43, Trade #4CB3A4, Transport #FBEB87, Finance #E090FD, Professional #9887C7, Public #ECF4FE, Other #6D747A); the residual pairs under the floors are recorded with the legend and the tile naming the class as the secondary encoding (round 10, C7). Rejected: ColorBrewer qualitative sets (round 9's first nine) and two levers the lab offered to meet the floors (drawing the Firms view's no-revenue pairs in the "below" tone, letting Transport go paler), not applied.

**What never takes colour.** The chrome's neutrals: the map wrap's 1 px border, the header and footer bands, the ghost names (a tone one step above the land, a cold tint since round 12, the tile's ice at 30 percent since round 16), the hairline regional borders at the national zoom, the graticule, the grain. The accents never colour data, ramps or the red; the red never colours chrome; the rings that mark coordinate pairs shared by 100 or more firms take the ink at reduced alpha, a neutral, for the same reason (round 16); nothing on the map canvas is coloured by an interface role except the hovered region's fuchsia outline and the cursor ring, which follow the cursor. The region tint and the glow under the dot field were the field's own colours, pulled back twice by the author (8 and 6 percent) until they sat at the level of a visibility assist, "never a display"; round 14 retired both with the pixel field, which follows the video's slide and has neither.

## 3. Motion

**What moves, and when.** The camera (flights through one easing with cubic position and log-k zoom; every move zooms toward its subject, the destination converging on the centre from the first frame, the author's choice of 24 October over a pan-then-zoom), the pulse of newly listed firms (round 24, the author's pick E of 29 October 2026: a sweep crosses the view west to east by longitude in 1.5 s, each red mark swells by 7.5 to 18 px and settles back over 0.9 s as it passes, and the year's minimum spanning tree by distance traces behind the sweep as thin red lines on a stage-coloured casing, each held 0.3 s and gone over 0.6 s, so a line lasts as long as the swell at its east end; once per forward year change and on the pulse button, 2.4 s in all; the pulse button's tooltip and label say the lines join the firms by distance only (the author's choice over a line on the legend's reading side, which is already tall); after the bloom's flash, glow and rings, round 15's single wide ring and round 22's swell alone, whose few pixels were lost at the country view), the year crossfades (presentation, never interpolation of values), the wire's scroll at 60 px per second, the strip's crossfade and the ticking figures of the tile footer, the minimap's leaders drawing on once at load, the slider thumb's brackets opening 2 px on hover and drag, the chart lines' reveals; since round 14 the Firms view's opening sweep (once per browser tab, the first time the view opens at the country view, each pixel and listed mark fading in as an invisible edge passes west to east over 1.3 s; any input completes it; never under reduced motion), the year dissolve (0.25 s, a crossfade, kept under reduced motion), the Cells view's pulse (a red dot that swells and goes at the centre of each cell holding a firm listed that year, cased in the stage colour, 1.5 px on the dark theme and 1 px on the light) and the pulse of a firm found by name. The principle (round 1, held since): motion only reveals data or moves the camera; discrete labelled annual snapshots; camera moves are pure functions of time so a frame is reproducible. Rejected: window dragging for the three map overlays (25 October: "No window dragging"; the mix box, the note and the legend read, none acts). One motion neither reveals data nor moves the camera, added at the author's request (General 7, round 16; the pop-out's fade the precedent): the REFERENCES panel grows from its centre line as it fades in over 180 ms and closes in 140 ms, transform and opacity only, at once under reduced motion.

**Names on the ground (round 16, the author's item 8, picked as 8Gb).** The 22 city names are laid out once at the country view and grow exactly with the land as the camera zooms (s = k / kFit, size, tracking and offset alike), so each keeps its place, as names printed on a map do; a name fades once it is wider than a third of the map, so none lies over the dots at city zoom, and a name that does not fit at the country view enters at the first zoom where it fits. Rejected: names at one screen size at every zoom (round 12's floating names, 8A), names that grow faster than the land (round 15's cloud pass, 8F), every name placed at the fit at smaller sizes (8Ga), and any extrusion or depth effect.

**The reduced-motion rule.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` flights jump, the pulse is a still highlight held for the same 1.8 s, the wire does not scroll and its items crossfade one at a time, the leaders do not draw on, the brackets do not open, the strip swaps without transition; no loop runs. Every motion has a still form that carries the same information.

**The one glow.** The flat-colour rule of round 10 (T3c): no shadow, gradient, filter, blur or backdrop effect anywhere on either page, with one exception, the data wire's phosphor glow (two drop shadows on the stationary track window, off on the light theme and under the contrast settings), the author's final choice of 26 October 2026; what stays besides is translucency (the atlas panels at 92 percent, the scale bar at 85, the pop-out at 95, the video's panels at 0.92 and the close panel at 0.6). The pulse lost its glow, swell and pale core in round 15 (the author's items 4 and 7B): the mark fades in to its red over 0.35 s and one thin ring eases out and fades, with the stagger and timing kept; under reduced motion one ring held still for 1.8 s; the year run plays it at each forward year; the video keeps round 9's bloom. The glow under the dot field at the national zoom (a blurred copy of the field at 6 percent, a visibility assist) was retired in round 14 with the pixel field.

**The wire's continuity.** The context strip of round 9 became a data wire of short dated true statements in round 10 and, in round 11, a queue that scrolls on through every context change: a change of region, layer or view appends the new cycle after the items queued and replaces only sets that have not entered the view; a year change rebuilds under a 0.3 s crossfade, since every item names the year. The principle: a running line never jumps because the viewer moved the cursor. Rejected: the CSS keyframe loop of round 10, which rebuilt and restarted the track at every change. Round 16 (the author's report of 27 October 2026 of a stutter while the cursor crossed regions): each region change had cancelled and restarted the scroll's animation twice; now one long animation runs to a far end, the items that leave are replaced by a spacer, and the slot's width change moves the track's margin, so no hover stops the scroll (measured in the author's Brave: 4.2 and 4.5 s held still in a 12 s sweep before, none after). The first slot shows the hovered region at once (wsA, the author's pick); rejected: a slot that waits its turn with the queue (wsB).

## 4. Text

**The three tones.** Ink #ECEEF1 (12.4:1 on the stage) for text that carries the content, muted #B4C0D0 for the second voice (sentence lines of legends, provenance in panels, the dissertation title), dim #7D889C for the smallest provenance and the footer. The muted tone was lifted one step on 25 October (from #9EABBD; the light theme's #52636D by the same OKLab step), the ink and the dim kept; the contrast table was re-run and the legend note and the METHOD block shown before and after. Rejected: pure white for body text. The ink has been #ECEEF1 since the charcoal palette of round 3; pure white appears only as a live variant (the GO TO card's hover under the ink-neutral proposal, superseded by the ice blue), never as a resting tone.

**The wording standard.** The chapter's own vocabulary: a statement is available or absent, a firm is removed from the registry, "listed" and "firms on no list"; "designation" only for the sanctions file's dated act; "(treated)" and "(comparison group)" as atlas-only glosses defined once on the page (24 October; "(designated)" declined because four of the five lists are undated memberships); never "supplied list", "treatment group", "sanctioned or strategic" or "control" on screen (the credits' caveat "Supplied lists are fixed memberships, not dated designations." is the recorded exception where provenance belongs); geocodes are house-level, street-level and other; pile counts are "distinct coordinate pairs"; shared registrations are explained in the author's three sentences with "can", not "often". Mechanics: no em-dashes and no semicolons on screen, one decimal place for percentages, named years, active voice with neutral subjects, every rate with its denominator, one message per scene in the title line, caveats once where they first matter. Firms on the five lists are named from the list files (25 October), firms on no list never, and the neighbour firm stays anonymous.

**Additions of 26 and 27 October 2026 (round 15, the author's rules).** "Listed" is never used on screen for list membership, since it reads as a stock-exchange listing, and "listing" goes with it: "firms on the five lists (treated)", "firms on no list (comparison group)", "added to a list", "on a list from 23 February 2024"; kept are the single-list labels ("sanctioned-only firms", "sanctions-list members"), code identifiers, data keys, past records and the manuscript; "firms on no exceptions list" was declined (one of the five lists is an exemption list). Models, estimates and intervals are named in standard econometric terms, by the book ("adjusted odds ratios", "95% confidence interval", "standard errors two-way clustered by industry and region"), with no flourish from the author or the lab. The sandbox's register (P-C): a concrete statement of what the data show and how it moves under a named check is preferred to a bare qualifier such as "provisional"; the formal caveats stay in the dissertation. A profile never restates what the atlas shows; it carries a researched, sourced insight, audited by the author, and only statistics shown nowhere else. Round 16 adds the insights' rules: a source is cited by publisher, document type and date with its link, never by title; a text does not repeat the map's listing date, and names the act where its year differs; the tile's short form carries no date; a description never points to one firm on no list. The panel's definitions take the dissertation's wording where a term is shared, one or two sentences each, with their sources in the provenance and not on screen. In the wire every item is in capitals, firm names at 400, and a cat fact keeps sentence case after a cat's head (a filled head with eye holes, long whiskers and a nose, the green at 85 percent over the panel) in place of the mark.

**Additions of 25 October 2026 (round 13, the author's decisions).** A fact that carries two statements is two sentences, never one joined by a semicolon (the wire's facts, the fine-cell line). Wherever the strip, the tile or the wire names a division, it is the short label with the code, "Wholesale trade (46)", never the code alone; the full NACE title stays in the panel and the METHOD block. The strip's slot carries the essentials only, the region's name and the year in tracked capitals, and the facts ride the wire. Firm names stay as filed. The capitals in the wire stay.

**Units.** Lower case inside tracked capitals through a span, joined to their number with a non-breaking space ("(250 m)", "100 km cells", "20 km"); unit symbols are fixed forms.

**Capitals.** As in section 1: names and labels in tracked capitals, prose in mixed case; a place name is the only part of a fact sentence set in capitals.

## 5. Layout

**Role borders.** Every button carries a 3 px border in its role colour at rest and the live variant on hover, the pressed state the solid accent fill with the stage colour as text, disabled at 0.4; the side column's cards and the explainer's control bar 3 px; the map overlays (the legend, the tile, the mix box, the wire's top rule, the scale bar, the zoom box's frame) 2 px so the map stays quiet; the map wrap a 1 px neutral; the toggles take their parent's role (round 10, H11, corrected twice from the mock). Rejected: a 3 px left-edge bar inside a 1.5 px border as the role marker, the lab's proposal and the mock of 25 October, removed by the author "on any button or control, anywhere, permanently". Rejected earlier: a clipped corner, because `clip-path` clips the border and the focus outline and a pressed fill loses a corner against the card's square frame.

**Squared corners.** Zero radius on every button, panel, card, swatch, tooltip and the map wrap, the explainer's controls included; the one exception, with its reason: the listed-state glyphs and the minimap's dots stay round because they depict the round marks on the map.

**The grain.** One 128 px thresholded noise tile as a second background layer on every chrome surface (the header and footer bands, the cards, the legend, the tile, the mix box, the wire, the scale bar, the zoom box, the explainer's controls, header and footer), never on the map canvas and never in the rendered video; its lift and drop are equal (about 6 levels on the dark theme, 4 on the light) so the measured contrast of text on a grained patch stays within 0.05 of the plain surface, which the audit guards (round 10, G22). Rejected: the first overlay-blend tile at 2.5 percent, invisible on the dark panel (sub-LSB).

**The collapse convention.** One square toggle (26 by 24 px, the parent's role colour, the glyph in the neon green, the fuchsia focus corners, `aria-expanded`) at the top right of every title row: the legend, the note, the mix box, the wire, the six right-column cards (the author's addition of 25 October); collapsed, a card shows its title row only; every state is remembered per viewer in local storage, with defaults by width (all cards open; the legend collapsed on a map under 600 px; the note open on a map 600 px wide or more and collapsed under it since round 15, the viewer's own toggle winning).

**The title bars and the i (round 15).** Every panel's title row reads [title][i][toggle] and sits on a bar across the panel inside its border, the panel's top layer: #020307 on the dark theme (halfway from the stage to black), #EFF2F5 on the light (the green title at 4.55:1), each with its own grain balanced on its tone; on GO TO the bar lies over the graticule, which runs on under it; a collapsed panel is its bar. The i is always in the neon green; its reading block opens under the title row, closed by default and remembered, with section heads in bold capitals; a panel without a method gets practical tips; the model outputs' blocks end with THE QUESTION, how the chart bears on the chapter's question.

**The LAYER card (round 15, C revised).** VIEW and the group names (COUNTS, STATEMENTS, REVENUE) share one column at the left, the width of STATEMENTS; the layer buttons are one width, their labels at the left; the red button, its counter and BLINK share one line, and in a year with no firm added the counter and its sentence dim with the disabled button (the author liked it).

## 6. Data honesty

**Every figure traceable.** Each number on screen comes from a checked pass with a provenance record (`motion/data/*.provenance.json`), and every string is a template over named fields of the shipped data; the wire's items are re-derived item by item by an independent checker that shares only the data with the generator (round 10, W14); the manifests carry their checks; no number computed in the lab is copied by hand into a page. The passes are deterministic and reproducible from pinned sources (188 entries in `config/sources.json`). Round 16's comparison at coordinate pairs shared by 100 or more firms is computed in the atlas from the address files, and its i text says the chapter does not report it; the state-related layer shows one classification, since unitary enterprises are largely the same firms seen through legal form (the author's revision), and its i text says so in the chapter's words. The city windows' shares read the windows' own totals since round 15 (the earlier sum of their cells left out the firms of withheld cells and read 1.4 to 17.4 points low; verified three ways).

**Descriptive wording.** Absence from the archive is not withdrawal or concealment; the lists are fixed memberships, not dated designations; associations are descriptive, never causal ("Paths compared, no effect estimated."); the 2025 extension is flagged; no regional reading is put on the change map; disclosure rules stand (shares need 50 firms, list counts under 5 per cell are suppressed, fine-cell counts under 10 are banded). The chapter treats membership as fixed and undated, so the dated turning-red is its own descriptive layer, said so in the record (25 October).

**The Groups card (round 24, the author's picks of 29 October 2026).** A table with its change drawn: each group's name, its 2021 and 2024 shares as figures, and a bar from one vertical zero line shared by every row (a range frame from the first bar to the last, drawn over the bars), so the changes compare by length; the change signed in points in the rise or fall colour; rows from the largest rise to the largest fall, state-run directly under state-related, every name on one left edge (pick A's hairline bracket removed on the author's word); series names in lower case, as the column's other charts; bars and line on whole device pixels; on a narrow card, where the bar strip would keep under 56 px (phones under about 400 px), each name on its own line above its figures. Rejected: the slope chart, whose labels slid off their dots when values lay closer than a line of text (round 24's report), and arrows between the two shares, which started at different places.

**Identity never by colour alone.** The legend and the tile name every class; the residual colour pairs under the validator's floors are recorded with that naming as the secondary encoding; the listed states carry glyphs (solid, hollow, rimmed) and the listed firms their names; the ramps are checked for colour-vision separation and the accents for distance from the red; the chart series are labelled.

**The audit habit.** Nothing ships on a prediction: the atlas audit at 400, 768, 1,200, 1,600 and 2,000 px on both renderers must report zero findings (canvas text boxes, DOM boxes, cameras, the colour rules, the wire, the layouts of a hidden-frame load), the video's text audit zero plus the counted lines, the palette validator for every colour, frame costs before and after, frames to the author at every checkpoint, and the author's word before every publish and the final commit of a round. Since 26 October 2026 the 1,600 px audit runs while building, and the full five-width run, `motion/tools/audit-five.sh`, runs at two gates: before a review goes to the author and before a publish; a publish needs one full run on the final build with zero findings, and a running audit is never stopped to fold in changes. Since round 16 the publish set ships the fonts the page loads and refuses any link that is not an insight's or a cat fact's source or one of the page's own. Since round 18 (the author's rule of 27 October 2026) `motion/tools/change-proof.py` proves a change against the build before it: a refactor must come out identical and needs no frames, and a change whose differences stay in the panels holding its strings, never on the map, is reviewed from the crop sheet without the review gate's five-width run. The publish gate's full run stays. Since round 19 (the author's rule of 28 October 2026) a change whose differences stay inside the header or the footer is reviewed from the crop sheet plus frames of any new hover or focus states with no review-gate audit, and any other review-gate audit runs only at the widths the change can affect, 1,600 and 400 px at least. The publish gate is unchanged.
